# Lumen — design system

This file is the source of truth for every screen. If a choice is not covered here, add it here first, then build it. Product context is in `PRODUCT.md`.

## 1. Concept: the instrument

Lumen looks like a precision instrument for light: the engraved scale of a lens barrel, the dial of a light meter, a Braun clock face with its one yellow second hand. Matte near-black, ivory markings, hairlines, and a single lit needle.

Three ideas carry it:

- **Scale and needle.** Position is shown by a hairline scale and a thin vertical needle in the accent colour, never by a round knob. The seek bar, the volume control, the equaliser, the tuner and every slider share it. Ticks stand on the scale's line, as they do in the brand mark.
- **House lights.** While a film plays, chrome is on a dark "stage" layer that melts away 0.4 s after the pointer stops (two seconds in the brief; the user asked for 0.4 s with an eased fade, 2026-11-14) and returns on movement. Nothing on the stage is boxed; controls sit on a scrim that has no visible edge.
- **Lit when on.** A control that is switched on shows its icon at full ink with a short accent bar lit beneath it, like an indicator lamp. The state reads by form, so it survives any accent, including a colourless one.

What it is not: cards in grids, cards inside cards, glass panels, glows, gradients on text or surfaces, side-stripe borders, bouncy motion.

## 2. Colour

All colour is OKLCH. Neutrals are tinted toward the accent hue (`--tint-h`, which follows the user's accent), with chroma 0.002–0.012, so nothing is pure grey, black or white. `src/design/color.ts` is the source of every number here; `tokens.css` mirrors it and a test keeps them in step.

### Themes

Dark is the default; light is available; "Match system" follows macOS. The stage (everything drawn over video: title bar while playing, control bar, on-screen display, PiP controls) always uses the dark tokens in both themes.

| Token | Dark (L C) | Light (L C) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `void` | 0.11 0.004 | 0.11 0.004 | Stage backdrop when there is no picture; the scrims |
| `bg` | 0.155 0.006 | 0.965 0.006 | Window base, home, settings |
| `surface-1` | 0.19 0.007 | 0.985 0.004 | Panels, sidebar, inspector |
| `surface-2` | 0.225 0.008 | 0.995 0.002 | Popovers, menus, tooltips, palette |
| `surface-3` | 0.27 0.009 | 0.935 0.007 | Resting fill of secondary buttons, hover fill, selected rows |
| `surface-4` | 0.31 0.009 | 0.90 0.008 | Hover and pressed fill on top of `surface-3` |
| `line` | 0.31 0.009 | 0.885 0.008 | Hairlines, dividers (decorative) |
| `line-strong` | 0.56 0.010 | 0.60 0.010 | Field and control outlines, scale tracks (3:1) |

Depth in dark comes from lighter surfaces, not shadows. In light, popovers also get one soft shadow.

### Accent

One accent, chosen by the user. Default **Limelight**, hue 106: the pale yellow-green of a limelight, the stage lamp the name comes from.

The accent has two roles, and they are different colours in the light theme:

- **`accent`, the ink.** The needle, lamps, the focus ring, accent text. In dark it is the bright colour: chroma 0.155 where the hue can carry it inside sRGB, at the lightest value that allows it. Only yellows may reach lightness 0.90; other hues are held to 0.82 so greens and cyans do not turn neon. Limelight is `oklch(0.90 0.155 106)`. In light it is a dark ink of the same hue, lightness 0.50, so thin marks stay legible on paper.
- **`accent-fill`, the fill.** The primary button. It is the bright colour in *both* themes, with `accent-fg` (`0.21 0.03`) text on it, so a light window gets a lit button rather than a dark slab. The button carries a 1 px ring in the accent ink, which gives it an edge on light and vanishes into the fill on dark. `accent-fill-hover` is 0.04 lighter in dark and 0.035 deeper in light.
- `accent-soft` is the selection fill: `0.30 0.045` in dark, `0.92 0.05` in light.

Presets: Limelight 106, Tungsten 78, Ember 40, Signal 25, Rose 5, Orchid 330, Iris 285, Azure 245, Lagoon 205, Jade 165, and Silver (no chroma). A hue slider gives any other.

The accent is rare. It marks the needle, lamps, keyboard focus, the current item in a list, and at most one primary button per view. Played progress is ivory, not accent. Because a user may choose an accent near red, nothing relies on the accent's hue to carry meaning.

### Status

`danger 0.72 0.17 25` · `warning 0.82 0.14 62` · `success 0.80 0.14 152` in dark; `0.52 0.19 25` · `0.52 0.12 62` · `0.50 0.13 152` in light. Status is never colour alone: it always carries an icon or words.

### Over video

- The two scrims are gradients of `void` that follow one smooth curve (smootherstep), so there is no line where the darkness starts. The bottom one is 156 px long and starts at 0.86 alpha; the top one is 96 px and starts at 0.78. An element painted with a scrim must be that tall (`h-scrim-bottom`, `h-scrim-top`). They are the only large translucent surfaces, and they are no longer than the text needs: the picture comes first.
- The scrims back text and icons only. Lines do not rely on them: the timeline's track and every needle on the stage carry a 1 px outline of `void` (`stage-edge`), so they stay drawn over a pure white picture with no scrim at all.
- The on-screen display appears without the chrome, over whatever the picture is showing, and must not dim the picture to do it. Its letters and its scale are outlined instead, the way subtitles are: a line of `void` around every shape (1 px at the smallest size, growing with the type) and a soft shadow beyond it. Over a dark picture the outline disappears; over a pure white one the letters are `ink-1` on `void` (see §7).
- Stage text is `stage-ink` (`ink-1`); secondary text and idle icons are `stage-ink-2` (0.72 alpha). Text lives in the transport row, within 46 px of the bottom edge, and the title within 32 px of the top, where the scrims keep it at 4.5:1 over a pure white picture.
- The unplayed timeline track is `stage-track` (0.52 alpha) inside its outline; the buffered range is `stage-buffer` (0.70).
- Hover fill on the stage is `stage-hover` (0.10 alpha), pressed `stage-press` (0.16).
- While the picture is shown in HDR its highlights can be several times brighter than the interface's white, and no scrim can hold them back. Then the stage chrome's letters and icons take the on-screen display's outline and shadow as well, so they keep an edge of `void` against any highlight. In SDR they do not need it and stay plain.
- One backdrop blur, and only one: behind the command palette (asked for by the user, 2026-11-14). The page behind it is blurred in CSS and the picture natively (an `NSVisualEffectView` between the video layer and the web view, blending within the window), under a `stage-veil` of `void` at 0.58, so the film goes on playing beneath, dimmed and soft. Nowhere else: the video is a native layer beneath the web view, so CSS blur cannot sample it, and near-opaque surfaces are calmer.

### Contrast

Body text meets 4.5:1 on every surface it can sit on, icons and control outlines 3:1. A unit test computes this for both themes and every accent preset, and for stage chrome over a white picture; it fails the build if a token change breaks it.

## 3. Typography

One family, **Plus Jakarta Sans** (variable, bundled), across the whole interface. The brief fixes the family, so hierarchy comes from size, weight, tracking and colour.

| Token | Size / line | Weight | Tracking | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `display` | 30 / 36 | 600 | −0.02em | Home headline, empty states |
| `title` | 22 / 28 | 600 | −0.015em | Window and settings page titles |
| `heading` | 17 / 24 | 600 | −0.01em | Panel titles, group headings, palette input |
| `body` | 13 / 20 | 420 | 0 | Everything else, including stage timecodes |
| `caption` | 11 / 16 | 500 | +0.01em | Metadata, tooltips, timestamps in lists |
| `osd` | 20–44, 3.4% of window height | 600 | −0.01em | The on-screen display only |

- Sizes are fixed `rem`; this is an app, not a page. Body is 13 px because that is macOS's own body size and what sits comfortably beside native menus and dialogs; the whole interface can be enlarged to 200% (§9). Steps are at least 1.29× apart except caption to body, the macOS convention, which is separated by weight and colour as well.
- Three weights carry every role: body (420, or 450 on light) for text, 500 for controls and captions, 600 for headings and emphasis.
- The one exception is `osd`. It confirms a key press to someone who may be two metres from the screen, so it scales with the window.
- `label` is `caption` in uppercase at 600 with +0.08em tracking. Use it only for short group labels in panels ("CHAPTERS"), never for sentences.
- Word spacing is opened by 0.07em everywhere: the family's own word space is only 0.17em, which runs words together at 13 px.
- Common ligatures are off. Browsers drop them wherever tracking is set, so "file" would otherwise be drawn two ways.
- Emphasis inside body is weight 600, not colour.
- Light theme body weight is 450; light text on dark reads heavier, so dark uses 420.
- **Numbers that change or align are tabular** (`.tabular`): timecodes, durations, percentages, decibels, sizes, speeds. Timecodes use `0:42:17` form, hours only when the media is an hour or longer.
- Text never wraps past 68ch. A film is called by its file's name without the extension, as the Finder shows it (the title bar, lists, tiles, messages); the whole name, extension and all, is in the tooltip and in the Finder. Names truncate in the middle, keeping their last ten characters, because the end is where one episode differs from the next, and show the full name in a tooltip when something is cut. A name whose weight changes (the row that becomes the one playing) is measured again. Truncation never splits an emoji or a combined character.

## 4. Space and layout

4 px base. Use the Tailwind scale only at these stops: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 (`1 2 3 4 6 8 12 16 24`). Siblings are spaced with `gap`, never margins.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `titlebar-h` | 44 px | Title bar; traffic lights start at x 20 and are centred on its height |
| `titlebar-lead` | 88 px | Kept clear at both ends of the title bar: the traffic lights on one side, and the same on the other so the title stays centred |
| `controlbar-h` | 84 px | Timeline row 24 over transport row 44, with 6 px above and 10 px below |
| `gutter` | 20 px | Stage side padding, aligned with the traffic lights |
| `sidebar-w` | 232 px | Library sidebar |
| `inspector-w` | 348 px | The panel beside the picture |
| `palette-w` | 600 px | Command palette |

- Rhythm: 4–8 inside a control group, 12–16 between groups, 24–32 between sections, 48+ around a page's lead element. Do not use one value everywhere.
- Rows that share a line share a height: 28 (compact rows, menu items), 32 (controls, fields), 36 (stage buttons), 44 (transport row, list rows with thumbnails).
- Flex rows that can be squeezed: label-sized items are `flex-none`, the one stretchy item is `min-w-0 flex-1`, text that may shrink gets `truncate`, labels that must not wrap get `whitespace-nowrap`. A row with a centred item and unequal sides is a grid with two `1fr` columns, and padding goes inside the cells.
- One left edge: the gutter. The traffic lights, the timeline, the first icon of the transport row and everything on the home screen start at 20 px.
- The transport row has three groups on the left, 12 px apart, with 4 px between controls inside a group: transport (Previous, Play, Next, Stop; Stop, asked for on 2026-11-14, closes what is open and goes back to Home, on ⌘. too, and is drawn as a filled rounded square like the other transport glyphs), volume (Mute and its scale), and the time readout, followed by the speed while that is not 1×. Previous and Next are in the row only while the playlist holds more than one item: a control that could never do anything is not shown. Then a spacer, then at most five controls on the right: the subtitles' pair (on and off, then the track menu), Cast, More and Full screen, always in that order and always ending in Full screen. The subtitles' pair and Cast are 12 px apart from each other and from More, as separate groups. Everything else lives in the More menu, the panel beside the picture and the palette. The row is drawn 9 px wider than the timeline at each end, so that the ink of the first and last icon, not their hover boxes, meets the ends of the track.
- Minimum window 560 × 360, and with the panel open 560 more than the panel, so the picture always keeps that much. The row answers to the width of the stage, not of the window. When the stage is too narrow the row gives way in this order: below 780 px Cast leaves the row for More (with a playlist's Previous and Next, Stop and a speed readout it no longer fits); below 720 px the volume scale folds into Mute and, on hover or keyboard focus, opens beside it over the readouts, which fade while it is open, so that nothing in the row moves; below 640 px the right-hand group keeps only More and Full screen, and what leaves it (the subtitles' pair) is listed in More; below 600 px the time readout drops its second figure. Nothing overlaps and nothing becomes unreachable: whatever leaves the row stays in the menus and on its keys.
- The trailing end of the title bar holds one control: the one that opens the panel beside the picture, where inspectors are opened on the Mac. While the panel is open that control is at the same corner, in the panel's own header. On the home screen, where there is no panel to open, the same place holds Lumen's own update while there is one (§7, "Updates").

### Shape

Radii: 4 (needles, ticks, chips, keycaps), 6 (buttons, fields, rows), 10 (popovers, menus, thumbnails), 14 (palette, sheets). Hairlines are 1 px `line`, drawn on whole pixels. In dark, overlays have no shadow unless they are opened from the stage, where they float over a moving picture; the command palette always has one.

## 5. Motion

- Two durations: `fast` 150 ms and `base` 200 ms. Nothing that responds to the user is slower. What the user summons (a menu, the palette, a tooltip, a panel) enters at `base` and leaves at `fast`. What returns on its own account (the stage chrome, the on-screen display) comes back at `fast`, because it is answering the user, and leaves at `base`. Hover and press feedback is `fast`.
- One curve: `ease-out` `cubic-bezier(0.25, 1, 0.5, 1)`. No bounce, no overshoot.
- Only `opacity` and `transform` animate; colour may cross-fade on hover and press. Height changes use grid rows.
- Chrome on the stage fades in over `fast` on pointer movement and, 0.4 s after the pointer stops, out over `leave` (500 ms on `ease-leave`, `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`: a soft start and a long settle, so it melts rather than blinks); the traffic lights fade with it on the same curve. `leave` is the one duration longer than `base`, because nothing waits on it. Movement means travel: the pointer must go 4 px, so a nudge of the desk does not raise it. The cursor hides with the chrome. A click on the picture does nothing (the user asked for that, 2026-11-14): Space, K and the Play button play and pause; a double-click goes in or out of full screen, and a press dragged 4 px moves the window.
- The chrome stays while a control is being dragged, a menu or popover is open, keyboard focus is on a control, or the film has ended. A pointer resting on the chrome itself (the title bar, with the traffic lights, or the control bar) keeps it up for as long as it rests there while paused, and for 3 s while the film plays, so the pointer left on Play after clicking it does not pin the chrome for the whole film.
- A paused picture with the chrome gone is deliberate. A film is often paused to be read (a subtitle, a slide, a frame), and the chrome would sit on exactly that.
- Panels slide 16 px and fade; popovers and tooltips move 4 px toward their control and fade; the palette scales from 0.98. The panel beside the picture is the exception: it pushes in and out by its whole width at `base`, and the picture glides aside with it (§7, "Panel beside the picture"). The window's frame is not animated: a frame that animates makes the page lay out on every step.
- Toasts arrive and leave at these two durations and on this curve like everything else, and a stack closes up without animating heights.
- Work in progress with no known length is shown by a needle sweeping a hairline, 900 ms each way on a symmetric curve. The hairline is 20 px in a button; on the stage it is 2 em of the on-screen display size, so it is seen from the sofa too. It is the one looping animation, and it only runs while something is actually working.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: every transform is removed, including press and hover scaling, and fades shorten to 100 ms; the working indicator rests at its centre. The timeline needle still moves; that is information, not decoration.

## 6. Iconography

`lucide-react`, 18 px on the stage and in toolbars, 16 px in menus and lists, stroke 1.75. Icons are `ink-2` at rest and `ink-1` on hover and when on. The transport glyphs (play, pause, previous, next) are filled, and play is shifted 1 px right to sit optically centred; use them from `components/ui/glyphs.tsx`, not from lucide directly. An icon never appears without an accessible name.

## 7. Components

Standard elements are shadcn/ui (Radix) primitives restyled to these tokens. Nothing ships with shadcn's default look.

**Button.** Heights 28 and 32, radius 6, body text at 500. Variants: `primary` (accent fill with its ring; one per view), `secondary` (`surface-3` fill with a `line` border), `ghost` (no fill until hover), `danger` (danger text; its label names what is destroyed). States: hover and pressed step the fill to `surface-4`, pressed also scales to 0.97, focus shows the ring, disabled is 0.45 opacity. Busy swaps the label for the working indicator, keeps the button's width and name, and ignores activation.

**Icon button.** 32 × 32 (36 × 36 on the stage), radius 6, ghost. Always has a tooltip. On: icon at full ink and an 8 × 2 px accent lamp beneath it. A control that cannot be used right now is `unavailable`, with the reason: it stays reachable and its tooltip says why. Plain `disabled` is only for controls that need no explanation.

**Overlay height.** A menu, submenu or popover is never taller than the room between its control and the window's edge: it keeps 8 px from the edges and stays below the title bar (52 px from the top, clear of the traffic lights), and a longer list (twenty subtitle tracks, every font) scrolls inside it with a thin scrollbar.

**Tooltip.** `surface-2`, 1 px `line`, radius 6, caption text: the action's name, an optional second line, then its shortcut in a keycap outline. It wraps at 68ch. Opens after 500 ms, instantly when moving between neighbours. It never takes the pointer: moving onto it closes it and a click passes through it. On the stage, tooltips of the transport row open above the timeline, on the line the hover timecode uses, so nothing covers the track. A control worked by more than one key shows each (↑ ↓). The trigger carries the name; the tooltip's spoken text is only the reason and the keys, and the control also exposes `aria-keyshortcuts`.

**Scale (slider).** A 2 px track of `line-strong`, filled portion `ink-1`, a needle 2 px wide and 14 px tall in the accent ink. The hit area is 24 px tall. Hover thickens the track. Round values are marks: 6 px ticks standing on the track, to which a *dragged* needle snaps within 6 px. The keyboard never snaps, so every value is reachable: arrows step, Shift+arrows step ×10, Home/End jump. A double-click on the needle resets to the default. Used with the pointer, it gives keyboard focus back when released. A mark steps aside (fades) while the needle is within 5 px of it, so the two never fuse into one shape. On the stage the track and the needle are outlined (§2). A scale whose value is not in effect, such as the volume while muted, dims its level and its needle. Panel scales may add `divisions`, 3 px graduations along the track. Every scale states its value in words with the unit (`valueText`).

**Scale row.** For panels: name (112 px, truncates), scale (stretches), value (56 px, tabular, right-aligned).

**Volume.** A Scale beside Mute. Ordinary levels, 0 to 100%, take the first three quarters of it and the boost to 200% the last quarter, so the normal range has room and boost is deliberate; the mark between them is 100%. It moves in steps of 5. Its tooltip names the level and follows the needle while it is dragged. Changing the volume by any means turns the sound back on. End goes to 100%, never to full boost.

**Timeline.** A bare Scale across the stage, gutter to gutter, above the transport row. Buffered range is `stage-buffer`. Chapters are 2 px gaps in the track; bookmarks are ticks standing on it; an A–B loop is an accent bracket. Hover shows a loupe: frame preview 192 px wide, timecode, and chapter name, clamped to the window edges, with a hairline on the track under the pointer. The preview is the keyframe at or before the pointer, which is what a click there will show first; until it arrives the loupe is the timecode and chapter alone, and it never changes size while the pointer moves. Audio, and anything that is not a file on this Mac, has no preview. While the needle is dragged the picture itself follows it, so the loupe shows no frame then. Dragging to the very end does not end the film while the button is still down; letting go there does. From the keyboard: ← → 5 s, ⇧← ⇧→ 1 min, Home and End. It never takes ↑ and ↓, which always mean volume. With keyboard focus the ring is drawn on the needle, not around the whole bar, and a timeline that cannot seek is not a tab stop.

**Time readout.** In the transport row after the volume group: `elapsed / total`. A click switches the second figure to time remaining, written `−1:19:28` with no slash before it, and a tooltip says what a click will do; the choice is remembered. The digits are for the eye: the readout's name is the same reading in words ("42 minutes 17 seconds of 2 hours 1 minute").

**Speed.** Not shown at 1×. At any other speed it stands after the time readout as `1.5×`, in `ink-1`, because a film running fast or slow is a state someone must be able to see; a click returns to 1×. It is set from the More menu, the palette and the keys `[` and `]`, in the steps 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2, 3, 4.

**Play.** One button: Play, Pause, and at the end of a film Play again, with a glyph for each.

**Menu and popover.** `surface-2`, radius 10, 1 px `line`, 4 px padding. Items 28 px tall, 8 px inline padding, radius 6; highlighted item is `surface-3`; checked items carry an accent check on the left, and a menu with any such item keeps that column for all of them, so names line up; in such a menu the column also holds each item's icon (16 px, `ink-2`; the action's own, and Speed `Gauge`, Repeat its mode's glyph, Cast `Cast`, Subtitle track `Languages`), so it is never empty space, and a switch that is on (Shuffle, Keep on top) shows the check in its icon's place (asked for on 2026-11-14); shortcuts sit right-aligned in `ink-3`. Submenus open to the side, never as a second popover over the first. A menu that is fading out is finished with: nothing in it follows the pointer or can be chosen again, so a double-click chooses once, and pressing its button brings it back. A menu is at least 180 px wide and no wider than 320; a name longer than that truncates. A popover that asks for something (a playlist's name) is a small form: label, field, and its actions at the bottom right with the primary one last.

**Subtitle controls.** In the transport row, a group of their own just before More: CC (on and off, lit while subtitles show, `C`; unavailable with the reason when the file has none) and the track menu (a languages glyph; its tooltip's second line is the track showing, or Off). The menu lists Off and each track, the one showing checked, with its format at the trailing end, then Add subtitle file… and the subtitle panel. On a tight stage (below 600 px) the pair leaves the row, and More starts with Show/Hide subtitles and a Subtitle track submenu. A film that has subtitles starts with them showing, even when no track is marked as the default or they are in the spoken language; turning them off is remembered for that file.

**Cast.** One button in the transport row, `Cast` from lucide (`Airplay` while an AirPlay session is on), lit with the accent lamp while a film is on a TV. Its tooltip names the device ("Playing on Living Room TV"). Its menu has two sections, each under its label: "Chromecast", then "AirPlay", listing the devices of that kind found on the network, each with what it is (its model; "Apple TV", "HomePod", "Mac", "TV" for AirPlay) in the trailing detail and a check on the one in use. A section with nothing found says "Looking for devices…" for six seconds, then "None found on this network", greyed. Below the AirPlay devices, "Other AirPlay devices…" opens the AirPlay picker of macOS from the same button, for a device the list does not have; the picker also opens by itself when a listed AirPlay device cannot be reached directly or wants a code, which only it can ask for. This Mac is never listed. Last, "Stop casting" while there is a session. Speakers are listed only for a file without a picture. The network is first searched when the menu first opens, not at launch, because that is when macOS asks whether Lumen may use the local network. While a film is on a TV, the paused frame on the Mac is dimmed by `void` at 70% and says, in the middle of the stage, "Playing on" in `stage-ink-2` body over the device's name in `stage-ink` title type, with a Cast icon of 32 px above; while the TV is still being reached the first line reads "Connecting to". When the film has to be converted for the TV as it plays, one caption line says so beneath. Every control in the bar, every key and the menus go on working and work the TV; speed and frame stepping do nothing while casting. Stopping leaves the Mac paused where the TV was. A failure is a toast that stays until closed, titled "Casting stopped", saying what happened.

**More menu.** What has no control of its own in the transport row: Speed and Repeat (each a submenu of its choices), Shuffle, Go to time…, Keep on top and the command palette, in that order, with a separator between kinds. It does not list what already has a control in sight: the panel beside the picture has its button in the title bar and its own tabs. When a control leaves the row in a narrow window it is listed here, first.

**Panel beside the picture.** On the right of the window, as wide as `inspector-w`, `surface-1` with a 1 px `line` on its left edge. It is drawn in the stage's tokens, dark in either theme: it stands beside a film, usually in a dim room, and a white panel would be the brightest thing there. What opens from it (menus, the form that names a playlist) is dark with it. It lies inside the window, in windowed mode as in full screen (asked for on 2026-11-14): the window keeps its frame, and the picture is fitted into what is left, never covered. It pushes in by its whole width, opaque, at `base` on `ease-out`, and the picture glides aside on the same curve and duration, the stage's controls following its edge, so the film shrinks with the panel instead of jumping; closing runs the same motion backwards, the picture taking its room back as the panel starts to leave. With reduced motion the panel fades in place and the picture moves at once. Only a window too narrow to leave the picture its minimum beside the panel grows, by the shortfall. While the panel is open the window may take any shape; once it closes the window is held to the picture's shape again. Its header is as tall as the title bar and drags the window like it: tabs on the left, 32 px tall like every control in the row, the active one in `ink-1` with an accent underline 2 px tall on the header's bottom edge, and the control that closes the panel at the trailing end. There are five tabs (Playlist, Chapters, Video, Audio, Subtitles), which is more than fit as words: they are icons of one size, 4 px apart, each with its name and shortcut as its tooltip and its name as its accessible name, and the name of the tab that is showing is the first line of its content, in `heading`. A tab whose lasting adjustments are in effect (the picture is tuned, the equaliser is on) carries a mark whether or not it is showing, so an adjustment made last week is never invisible. The lamp's own place beneath the tab is taken by the active tab's underline, so on a tab the mark is a 4 px accent dot at the icon's top right corner, and the tab's accessible name adds "in effect". Content is rows, not cards. It always starts closed; which tab it shows is remembered. Opened from the keyboard, it takes the keyboard to its list; opened with the pointer, the keys stay with the film.

**List row.** 28 or 44 px, radius 6, hover `surface-3`, selected `accent-soft`. On the selected fill, `ink-3` text is drawn in `ink-2`: tertiary ink does not reach 4.5:1 there in the dark theme. The current item shows an accent lamp at the leading edge of the row's content, at the same place in every list, and `ink-1` at 600. With keyboard focus the ring is drawn on the selected row, not around the whole list. The selection is where the keyboard is in the list: it is dropped when the pointer goes back to the picture, and in a list that acts on a click (Chapters) a click leaves none behind, so the lamp is never contradicted by a stronger mark. Reordering lifts the row under the pointer and shows a 2 px accent insertion line. Removing shows an Undo toast rather than a confirmation. In the playlist, a list that is all one show's episodes (as a torrent of a season is) is headed by the show when it has no name of its own, and each row reads as its episode, in a caption column as wide as the longest number ("S2 E6"), then the episode's own name ("Behind the Red Door"), never the release name with its quality, whose differing part a cut would hide; the whole release name is the name's tooltip and what a screen reader hears. Playing a file of a torrent makes every playable file of it the playlist, in order, starting at the one chosen, so the episodes before it are there too (asked for on 2026-11-14).

**Playlist.** The Playlist tab is the queue. A playlist that has a name (the saved one it was opened from or saved as) shows it as the header's heading; one without is headed "Playlist", as every tab is headed by its name. The count and total length stand beneath in `caption`. The header carries Shuffle, Repeat and a menu: Add files…, Save as…, Open playlist (a submenu of the saved ones, one level: choosing a name opens it), Rename… and Delete for a saved playlist that is open, and Clear. Repeat is named for what it is, with its mode (off, all, one) as the second line of its tooltip. Rows are 28 px: the position in the list in a column as wide as the longest position needs (the lamp, for the one playing, at its leading edge), the title truncated in the middle, the length in tabular figures on the right. A click selects; a double-click or Return plays; Delete removes, with Undo; a drag reorders, and ⌥↑ ⌥↓ do the same from the keyboard. On hover a row shows a control that removes it, where its length was. Files dropped on the panel are added where they are dropped; dropped anywhere else they replace the playlist and play. A long list is virtualised: only the rows in view are in the page. An item that would not play is stepped over; a toast says which and why, and afterwards its row is dimmed and says so in place of its length, until it plays.

**Video, Audio and Subtitles tabs.** Each is a column of groups under the tab's name, scrolling when it is taller than the panel. A group has a `label` heading in `ink-3`, with what acts on the whole group (Reset, a preset picker) at its trailing end, and its controls beneath as rows 32 px tall; groups stand 20 px apart. Three kinds of row cover everything. A continuous value is a Scale row; its reading at the end carries its sign and unit ("+12", "150%", "−0.25 s"), in `ink-1` when it differs from neutral and `ink-3` when it does not; a scale that goes both ways from a neutral has its mark there and is lit from the mark to the needle; a double-click on the needle resets that one value. One of several is a Picker: the name, then the present choice with a chevron, opening a menu in which the present choice carries the check. On and off is a Switch at the trailing end of its name. A group led by a preset Picker (Picture, Equaliser) is reset through it: its first choice is the neutral one ("Neutral", "Flat"), so it carries no separate Reset. Output carries none: each of its rows is one choice, set back by choosing again, and a Reset there would move the sound to another device. In any other group Reset appears only when something in the group that has a neutral (a delay, a turn, a crop; not which track is chosen) differs from it, and takes the keyboard's place of whatever it replaces nowhere: it is added and removed at the end of the heading row, so nothing below moves.

What belongs to the film and what stays is the rule behind the grouping. Tracks, delays, and how the picture is turned, cut and stretched belong to the file that is open: they start fresh with the next file and are remembered with this one, so it opens as it was left. The picture's levels, the equaliser, where sound goes and how subtitles look stay until they are changed, because they are about the room, the screen and the listener.

**Track list.** The audio and subtitle tracks of the open file, as rows 28 px tall in a radio group: the lamp at the leading edge of the one in use, then what a person would call it (its language in words, then its title if it has one), then its format in `ink-3` at the trailing end ("5.1 · Dolby Digital", "SubRip"). Subtitles lead with "Off". A track loaded from a separate file says so by that file's name.

**Sync to speech.** The last row of the Subtitles tab's Track group, shown once the file has subtitles. Its name and one Secondary button (small, 28 px) share a 32 px row, like any other; beneath, full width, a `caption` line in `ink-3` says what it will do, is doing or did, with a second caption line for anything more. The button does the one thing that makes sense at the moment: Sync, Cancel while it works, Undo once it has, Try again after a failure; it keeps one width (92 px) so the row does not shift as its word changes. While it works, a 2 px hairline under the captions fills from the leading edge, `ink-1` on `line-strong` (the scale's colours), the download first and then the samples heard ("Downloading the speech model… 42%", "Listening… 3 of 8", in tabular numbers). Before the first sync the second caption says a 60 MB speech model will be downloaded and runs on this Mac: nothing happens to the network that the person was not told about. The result is said in words in `ink-1` ("Shown 7.47 s earlier, retimed from 25 to 23.976 fps") with how sure it is in `ink-3` beneath ("7 of 8 samples agree"; "Matched by when people speak" when the subtitles are in another language than the sound); the Delay scale above moves to the delay found, so the result is also where the delay is always read. Subtitles that came inside the film cannot be synced: the button is unavailable and the caption says they are already timed with it. Failure is said in the caption, not in a toast, since it belongs to this row. The palette and the Subtitles menu have "Sync subtitles to speech", which opens the tab to show it working.

**Equaliser.** Ten bands, one to an octave from 31 Hz to 16 kHz, as vertical scales of one height standing in a row, each with its frequency beneath in `caption`; ±12 dB, with a hairline across all ten at 0. The needle is horizontal; the level is lit from the 0 line to the needle. Its heading carries the Switch that turns it on and off and the preset Picker; a set of gains changed by hand reads "Custom" and can be saved under a name. Switched off, the bands keep their places and dim.

**Visualiser.** A file with sound and no picture shows its sound instead of a black stage: the waveform as one continuous trace across the middle of the picture, like an oscilloscope's, in `ink-1` at 0.9 on black, the colour of the bars around any picture. It is a hairline, as the rest of the instrument is, never filled bars. It is drawn by the engine as the picture, so it is under the chrome like any film, and it can be turned off in the Audio tab (from the next file opened).

**Colour swatches.** For choosing a colour from a few: circles 20 px across, 8 px apart, the chosen one with a 2 px ring of `ink-1` standing 2 px off it. The last opens the system's colour panel for any other.

**Chapters.** The Chapters tab is the film's table of contents: each row its start time in tabular figures, then its name. A click goes there. The chapter being played carries the lamp. A film without chapters says so in one line.

**Field.** 32 px, radius 6, 1 px `line-strong`, `surface-1` fill, focus ring. Always has a visible label; placeholders are examples, not labels. Errors appear below the field with an icon.

**Switch, checkbox, radio, segmented control, tabs.** Radix primitives. On-state uses the accent; segmented control items are label-sized, not equal-width. A Switch is 28 × 16 with a 12 px knob: off, a `surface-4` track with an `ink-2` knob; on, an accent track with the knob in the accent's ink. In a row its name is its label, and a click on the name works it.

**Picker.** For one of several, where a Switch will not do and a scale would be a lie: a ghost button 28 px tall showing the present choice in `ink-1` and an up-down chevron in `ink-3`, 4 px apart, never wider than the room its row leaves (the choice truncates). It opens a menu of the choices, the present one checked, aligned to the button's trailing edge.

**Command palette.** ⌘K. 600 px wide (never wider than the window less its gutters), radius 14, `surface-2`, in the upper third of the window. Heading-size input, grouped results, each row an icon, a name and its shortcut. Recently used actions lead. Besides every action it finds what is at hand: the items of the playlist, the film's chapters, saved playlists and what was played recently. Before anything is typed it offers three of each of the last two, and of what was played, not the film that is open: that is not something to go back to. A time typed into it ("1:23:45", "90m", "25%") offers to go there. An address typed or pasted into it ("https://…", "vimeo.com/…") offers to play it, and to add it to the playlist when there is one. ⌘U, "Open address…", opens it for an address alone: a link icon in place of the magnifier, and nothing but the address's rows. ⌘V of a copied link anywhere outside a text field plays it, as a dropped file plays. An action that cannot run now is listed dimmed with the reason in place of its shortcut, and does nothing. Rows are 36 px; the list shows at most eight before it scrolls.

What it finds is ordered by how well it answers, not by kind: the kinds are still grouped, but the group with the best answer comes first, the best answer leads its group, and what cannot be done now sinks below what can, so Return always runs the best thing that is possible. A word typed matches the start of a name, the start of a word in it, or the beginnings of several of its words in order ("sp" for "Show playlist"); inside a word it matches only as typed. Actions also answer to the other words people use for them ("loop" for Repeat, "louder" for Volume up). An action is listed under the name it has now ("Pause" while a film plays). With nothing typed it shows what was used lately, saved playlists and what was played recently, three of each, and then every action that can be done now, by category; what cannot be done now is found by typing its name.

**Torrent search.** "Search torrents…" (⌘F, File menu, and the find field on the home screen, beside "Open file…") is the palette asking for a search alone, with a magnet in place of the magnifier. It searches the user's own server (default `https://iamapirate.duckdns.org`, `LUMEN_TORRENT_SERVER` overrides), which fetches through TorBox and streams the files back; nothing in Lumen links out to it. Nothing is searched until it is asked for: Return in the torrent view searches what is typed (a row "Search for “…”" with its Return keycap), and the results take the first row, so a second Return plays the best. Anything of three or more characters typed into ⌘K offers a row "Search torrents for “…”" after everything else found, which opens the torrent view and searches. Searches are remembered on this Mac (eight, newest first): with nothing typed the torrent view lists them, and ⌘K the last three, each list ending with "Clear search history". Only results TorBox already has are listed. Results are listed under "Ready to play": releases of 1080p or better (2160p, 4K, UHD) first, then the rest, each best shared first; each row is the release's name (cut at its end, since a release leads with its title), its size and its seeds in tabular columns. Choosing one keeps the palette open on its fetch: a label for the state ("Fetching", "Finding the files", "Waiting for someone to share it"), the name, a scale whose needle is the progress (the sweeping needle while it is unknown), then percent, speed and seeds. One playable file plays at once; several are listed to choose from (the one chosen plays with the files after it queued, so the next episode follows); a torrent with nothing Lumen plays opens its file in the browser to download, and what is playing goes on. While a film plays, the row in hand shows "Play next" in place of its seeds (⌘↩ does the same): the result is fetched and put straight after what is playing, to start when it ends or on Next. What is playing never stops for a search; only choosing to play replaces it. Closing the palette mid-fetch leaves it running, and a toast says when it is ready, with Play, Download or Choose.

"Go to time…" (⌘J) is the palette asking for a time alone, and takes whatever a person would type for one: "1:23:45", "83:45", "90m", "1h30", "1.5h", "25%", "+30" and "-2m" for a step from here, and a bare number, which it offers both ways (90 as minutes and as seconds), the likelier first.

**On-screen display.** One line at the top right of the stage, below the title bar's line so the two never meet, in `osd` type. It confirms a change that the visible controls do not already show: a label, a tabular value, and for ranges a scale. A key that can do nothing just now says why here ("This is the last item", "Already at normal speed"), in the words its control's tooltip uses, so no key press goes unanswered. It is drawn above the chrome and its scrims, with no panel and no scrim of its own; the outline (§2) keeps it readable over any picture. Its scale is sized in ems, so it grows with the type, and it tells the level by thickness as well as by the needle (the part up to the needle is twice as thick), because over a bright picture brightness cannot. A new message replaces the old one in place without fading in again, so holding a key never makes it flicker, and the needle glides to each new value. It fades in over 150 ms, stays 1.2 s after the last change, fades out over 200 ms, and is announced politely to VoiceOver. A change made with a control that shows the new state itself (clicking Mute or Play) does not also raise it. Steps through the film by key (← → J L ⇧← ⇧→) and the jumps on 0–9 do not raise it either (asked for, 2026-11-14): the picture jumping is the answer; only a step that can do nothing ("At the end") still says why. The digits 0–9 go to that tenth of the way through, as on the web's video sites (3 is 30%), listed under Playback ▸ Jump To. Play and pause never raise it, from any key or click: the picture moving or stopping is the answer, and a word in the corner for it reads as a player of another age. Pausing takes down any message that was showing.

**Skip recap / Skip intro.** While a chapter named as a recap ("Recap", "Previously on…") or an intro ("Intro", "Opening credits", "Title sequence"…) plays, a secondary button stands at the bottom right of the picture, at the gutter and 16 px above the control bar's line, saying "Skip recap" or "Skip intro" with a double chevron. It is only an offer: nothing is ever skipped by itself. It stays while the rest of the chrome dims (the moment to take it is now) and goes once there is under 1.5 s of the part left. Choosing it (or ⇧S, or Playback ▸ Skip Intro) goes quietly to the start of the next chapter.

**Next episode.** When what is playing is named as an episode ("MobLand S02E09 …", "show_s1e10", "Fargo 3x07"), Lumen reads the show and the numbers from the name. The torrent view and ⌘K, before anything is typed, lead with two groups, "Next episode · MobLand S02E10" and "Next season · MobLand S03E01" (for a season's last episode). Each is searched for as soon as either opens (once per episode, then remembered) and lists the ready releases of exactly that episode, best shared first (five in the torrent view, three in ⌘K), with Play next on the row in hand; while it searches, a sweeping needle and "Looking for …"; with none ready, "Nothing is ready to play for it yet.". Each group always ends with a "Find …" row that searches wider in the torrent view (⇧⌘F for the next episode, also File ▸ Find Next Episode). The first row is taken again as results arrive, so Return plays the next episode. Near the end it looks by itself, quietly: 30 s before a chapter of end credits starts, else a minute before the end, and not when the playlist already has something next. It searches for the next episode, and only if none is ready, for the next season's first; a result counts only if it is that very episode of that show (not a season pack). If one is found, a card stands where Skip intro does (bottom right, gutter, 16 px above the control bar's line, staying while the chrome dims): the label "Next episode found", the episode in `heading`, the release's name with its size and seeds in `caption`, then "Play next" (primary) and "Not now" (ghost), with a close control. Play next fetches it in the background and puts it after what is playing, and a toast says so; nothing is ever added without being asked. The card is offered once per episode.

**Toast.** Top right, under the title bar, where the Mac shows its own notifications; on the stage it starts below the line the on-screen display uses, so the two never meet, and with the panel open it is at the top right of the picture. Nothing else is drawn there: the loupe, the transport row's tooltips and the menus that rise from it are all at the bottom. `surface-2`, 356 px wide at most and never wider than the window less its gutters. For events with a follow-up ("Frame saved · Show in Finder", "Removed from the playlist · Undo") and for failures that have nowhere better to be said. Its first line says what happened in a few words; a name it concerns goes on a second line of its own, on one line, truncated. Every toast has a close control. One that reports that something asked for could not be done stays until it is closed; one that says something is being done (an update being looked for, or downloaded) stays for as long as that is so, since leaving on a timer would say it had finished; the others leave on their own, after 5 s, or 8 s if there is a second line to read, and that includes an item of the playlist being stepped over, which the film goes on past. Only the front toast of a stack shows its words. While the command palette or a menu on the stage is open the toasts step out of sight, so that nothing lies across it; one that is still due is there again afterwards. Escape closes the front toast; ⌘Z does what its Undo offers.

When a film starts where it was left, a toast says so and offers the other choice: "Resumed at 0:42:17 · Start over".

**Updates.** Lumen can replace itself with a newer version from its releases on GitHub. It looks for one by itself, five seconds after it opens and every six hours while it stays open, for as long as "Check for updates automatically" is on (it is until it is switched off), and whenever it is asked to ("Check for updates"). Looking is one small request. Nothing is downloaded until someone asks for it, and nothing is installed that does not carry the signature of Lumen's releases, checked against the key built into the app.

What Lumen finds by itself it does not announce: no toast, and nothing over a film. A newer version is one control at the trailing end of the home screen's title bar, where the stage keeps the panel's control: a ghost button 28 px tall in `ink-1`, led by a 6 px accent lamp, reading "Update to 0.2.8", its last letter at the gutter. Its tooltip says which version this is and that updating restarts Lumen. Choosing it turns it, in place, into a readout of the same height: "Downloading", the share in tabular figures in a column wide enough for "100%", and a scale 48 px long with the needle at the share (the sweeping needle while the size is not known, and for the moment of "Installing" that follows). There is nothing to press while it arrives. The control fades in once, when it first appears; after that what it says changes in place without motion, as a readout does. Once the new version is in place and nothing is open, Lumen starts again as that version at once: that is what was asked for, and there is nothing to interrupt. If a film has been opened by then it is left alone: the new version is simply what opens next time, and the control reads "Restart to update" for whoever wants it sooner.

Someone who asks is always answered, in one toast that each stage takes over: "Checking for updates…", then "Lumen is up to date" over "0.2.7 is the newest version", or "Lumen 0.2.8 is available" over "You have 0.2.7" with Update. Over a film, where the home screen's control is not in sight, an update that was asked for is followed in that toast: "Downloading Lumen 0.2.8" over "42% · 17 of 41 MB", "Installing Lumen 0.2.8", then "Lumen 0.2.8 is installed" over "It opens the next time Lumen starts", with Restart. On the home screen the control shows all of that, and a toast as well would say one thing twice. A failure (no connection, a download cut short, a file not signed as a Lumen release, a place Lumen cannot be replaced in, such as its disk image) is a toast that stays, saying what happened and what to do about it; what had been found is still offered, and asking again takes the failure away.

Lumen's own menu, the first in the bar, has them under About, where a Mac app keeps them: "Update to 0.2.8" (then "Restart to Update") only while there is one to choose, "Check for Updates", and "Check for Updates Automatically" with its tick. Everywhere else in the bar an item that cannot be chosen now is dimmed in its place; "Update Lumen" with nothing to update to, which is nearly always, would be a standing false promise, so it is listed only when it is true. None ends in an ellipsis: nothing more is asked before it acts. The palette lists the same three under "Lumen", after every other category, because what it lists first should be about the film.

**Empty state.** Teaches the next action. The home screen with no history is the instrument at rest, set as one column 34rem wide (narrower only when the window is): a graduated scale with the needle at its start, a headline ("Drop a file to play") 24 px under it, one line saying what Lumen plays, and 24 px under that the ways to start, on one line at one height (36 px): "Open file…" (the view's primary button) with its shortcut set inside it, dimmed, after the label, then a field that fills the rest of the column, "Find a film or a show, or paste a link", with a magnifier and its shortcut (⌘F) inside it. Return in the field plays a link (as pasting one anywhere does) and otherwise opens the torrent search on what was typed. The scale and the field share the column's right edge, so the lead reads as one instrument, not a strip across an empty window. When the column is too narrow for the field to keep 16rem, the field wraps under the button at full width. No shortcut stands outside its control as a separate keycap. "Open address…" (⌘U) stays in the File menu and the palette; the field covers it here. The whole window is the drop target; while a file is dragged over it, an accent hairline frames the window, the headline changes to "Release to play" and the rest steps back.

**Failure to open.** Said where the user is looking, and it stays: on the home screen the line under the headline becomes the failure, with a danger mark, naming the file and the reason in one sentence, with "Show in Finder" (ghost, 36 px) between "Open file…" and the field when the file's folder still exists. It is replaced by the next attempt. It is never a toast that leaves on a timer.

**Media tile.** A 16:9 thumbnail, radius 10, with the title and one line of metadata beneath it. No card around it. Resume progress is a 2 px scale along the thumbnail's bottom edge, drawn like the timeline: ivory for what has been played, with the needle at its end. The thumbnail is a frame from the film, the same one every time, so a film is recognised by it; sound without a picture, a stream, and anything whose frame is not there yet, shows a title card instead of an empty box: the name in `stage-ink` heading type, at most two lines, at the card's lower left with 12 px inset, under the episode ("S2 E5") as a `stage-ink-2` label when there is one. A release name is read as a person would write it: "MobLand.S02E05.1080p.WEB.H264-RAWR" is "MobLand", episode "S2 E5"; "A.Quiet.Hour.1962.1080p.BluRay.x264" is "A Quiet Hour (1962)"; a name with no release words is left as it is. The full file name is the title's tooltip. The metadata line leads with the episode when there is one ("S2 E5 · 47 min") and says what is left ("1 h 19 min left"), or the length when nothing is left to resume. One whose length is not known says that. A tile whose file is no longer where it was is dimmed and says so, and its menu offers only to remove it. A click plays, from where it was left; its menu offers Play from the beginning, Show in Finder and Remove from recently played, and Delete on a focused tile removes it, with Undo, and takes the keyboard to its neighbour. The focus ring is drawn on the thumbnail, clear of the caption. The progress scale is inset by the thumbnail's corner radius, so its needle is whole at both ends.

**Home, with history.** Once something has been played, the lead keeps its place at the top left (the scale, the headline, the ways to open) and what was played follows under the label "Recently played" (a `line` hairline runs from the label, 12 px after it, to 12 px before the word of a ghost "Clear" at the end of its line, whose word ends at the grid's edge; it forgets them all, with Undo), newest first, a playlist once (asked for on 2026-11-14): every item played in one is recorded with the playlist as it stood, and the tile is the item played last, its metadata line leading with a small playlist glyph and its place ("2 of 5 · 47 min"). Opening that tile, from the home screen, File ▸ Open Recent or the palette, brings the whole playlist back and plays that item, from where it was left; a saved playlist comes back under its name. Removing the tile forgets the whole playlist. Something played alone opens alone. A playlist's tile carries a chip at its thumbnail's top right (a playlist glyph and the count, `stage-veil` behind `stage-ink`, 24 px tall), and its menu "Show playlist": either lists the playlist without playing anything, in a popover beside the tile (under or over it where the window has no room beside), headed by the playlist's name or the show's, with the count. Each row is its place, the episode ("S1 E2", in a fixed column) and the episode's own name, with its length; the item played last has the needle in place of its number and a second line, "Last played · 1 h 19 min left", and the keyboard starts on it (↑ ↓ Home End move, Return plays). Choosing any row brings the whole playlist back and plays that item, from where it was left. Tiles are at least 168 px wide filling the window's width, six across at most (a wider window gets larger tiles, not more of them), two rows at most. Saved playlists follow under the label "Playlists", ruled the same way, as names with their count and length, no thumbnails: a click opens one, and its menu can delete it, with Undo. The line that says what Lumen plays gives its place to them: someone who has played a film knows.

**Pinning, removing, incognito** (asked for on 2026-11-15). Each tile has two small stage controls at its thumbnail's top left, the playlist chip's kind (24 px, `stage-veil` behind `stage-ink`, 4 px apart): Pin and Remove (an ×). They appear while the pointer is on the tile or the keyboard is in it; a pinned tile keeps its pin in sight, filled, and under the pointer it turns to "pin off", what a press would do. Remove forgets that tile (a whole playlist for a playlist's tile), with Undo, as Delete on a tile and its menu's "Remove from recently played" do. Pinned tiles come first, newest first among themselves, and "Clear" leaves them (and the rest of a pinned playlist); with only pinned tiles left, Clear is dimmed and says "Everything left is pinned". Incognito is a switch (File ▸ Incognito, ticked while on; the palette; ⇧⌘N; and a 36 px icon button with the lamp at the end of the home screen's row of ways to start, after the find field). While it is on nothing is written: not to Recently played, not where a film was left or what was chosen for it, not to the remembered torrent searches. What was remembered before is still read, so a film still resumes. It is a setting, so it holds across launches until turned off; the home screen says so under the row of ways to start, one caption line with the glyph: "Incognito: nothing you play or search for now is remembered." Over a film the switch confirms itself on the on-screen display.

## 8. Interaction

- Every action lives in the action registry with an id, a name, a default shortcut and a handler. The menu bar, the More menu, tooltips, the palette and the Shortcuts settings tab are generated from it. The menu bar shows the registry's shortcuts as its key equivalents, and the page hears a key before the menu bar does, so the one set of rules below decides what a key means wherever it is pressed.
- The menu bar is a Mac menu bar. Where the More menu offers one of several (Speed, Repeat), the menu bar offers the same choice as a submenu with the same names and a tick on the one in force; an action whose name follows state is listed under the name it has now (Play, then Pause; Enter Full Screen, then Exit Full Screen); and File has Open Recent, the last ten things played, newest first, with Clear Recently Played under them, and Open Playlist, the saved ones. The key that steps through the repeat modes is not listed there: the submenu names the three modes, and the key is on the Repeat button's tooltip and in the palette.
- Focus is always visible for keyboard users: a 2 px accent ring, offset 2 px (4 px on a needle, which is itself only 2 px wide), on keyboard focus only. A control used with the pointer does not keep the keyboard, so Space still plays and pauses and the arrows still seek.
- "Keyboard focus" has one definition: focus that was moved by a key press, recorded at the moment it moves, and given up when the control is next pressed with the pointer. The ring, the shortcut rules below and the auto-hiding chrome all read that one record, so they cannot disagree, and nothing depends on how a particular WebKit version guesses at `:focus-visible`.
- Global shortcuts always work, with two exceptions. In a text field, and inside an open menu, pop-up list or dialog, every plain key belongs to that thing; only chords with ⌘ or ⌃ and the function keys still reach the player, and of those not the ones that edit text (⌘← ⌘→ ⌘A ⌘C ⌘V ⌘X ⌘Z and their kin), which stay with the field. A control that has keyboard focus keeps just the keys its kind uses (Space and Return on a button; the arrows, Home, End and the Page keys on a slider); every other key still reaches the player, so a focused button never stops the arrows from seeking. ↑ and ↓ mean volume on the stage: the timeline does not take them.
- A list in a panel (the playlist, the chapters) is a control like any other: with keyboard focus it keeps the keys that work a list (↑ ↓ Home End the Page keys, Return, Delete) and leaves the rest to the player. Used with the pointer it keeps none of the player's keys, so Space still pauses after a row is clicked; Return and Delete, which the player does not use, still act on the row that was selected.
- Escape steps out one level at a time, innermost first: it closes whatever is open (tooltip, menu, palette, then the front toast); otherwise it gives the keyboard back to the picture, which lets the chrome go; otherwise it leaves full screen.
- ⌘Z undoes the last thing that offered Undo, for as long as its toast is up. In a text field it is the field's own undo.
- Tab reaches every control, whether or not the Keyboard navigation setting of macOS is on. While a control on the stage has keyboard focus, the chrome stays up.
- Sliders respond on drag, not on release. Seeking shows the frame under the pointer while dragging.
- Scrolling over the picture: up and down change the volume, a step for each 14 points the fingers travel; sideways moves through the film, about 12 seconds for each 100 points, and lands exactly when the fingers lift. A gesture is one or the other, decided by which way it starts, and it follows the fingers themselves, whichever way scrolling is set in macOS. The glide macOS adds after the fingers lift is ignored: the volume stops when the hand does. A wheel that turns in notches gives one step a notch. Over a control, a panel or anything open, scrolling is that thing's own.
- On the picture: a click plays or pauses, a double-click toggles full screen, a drag moves the window. A click takes effect after 150 ms, which still feels immediate; a second click inside that time makes it a double-click instead. A slower double-click undoes the pause its first click caused, so going to full screen never changes whether the film is playing.
- When a film ends, the chrome returns and stays until something else happens, with Play ready to start it again.
- Destructive actions prefer Undo to a confirmation. Confirmation is reserved for deleting files from disk.
- Lumen picks up where things were left. A film of ten minutes or more that was stopped part way starts again three seconds before that point and says so; anything shorter, and anything played to its last 3%, starts from the beginning. The volume, mute, shuffle and repeat are as they were; a boost above 100% is not carried over to the next run.
- When an item of the playlist ends, the next one plays. Opening one file plays that file and then stops on its last frame: Lumen does not go on to whatever else is in the folder. A folder, several files, or a playlist file opened together are a playlist.
- Stacking order, low to high: stage chrome, inspector, popover, palette, toast, tooltip.

## 9. Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA. Everything is reachable by keyboard in a sensible order. Icon buttons have names and `aria-keyshortcuts`, sliders have `aria-valuetext` in words ("42 minutes of 2 hours 1 minute"), toggles expose pressed state, unavailable controls stay focusable and explain themselves, the on-screen display is a polite live region. `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-contrast: more` are honoured; the latter promotes `ink-3` to `ink-2` and `line` to `line-strong`, on the stage as well. The interface scales from 100% to 200% (View → Larger, Smaller, Actual size), and every layout must hold at 200%.

## 10. Voice

Sentence case everywhere in the window. A control that switches between two states is named for what choosing it will do now (Mute, then Unmute; Show playlist, then Hide playlist; Close file, or Close window when no file is open), and its menu items and palette row say the same. One with more than two states is named for what it is, with the state it is in as the second line of its tooltip (Repeat: Off, All, One). Name the thing and the action: "Open file…", "Add bookmark", "Burn in subtitles", "Next item". The menu bar is the system's own surface, where Lumen's items sit beside the system's (Hide Others, Select All, Emoji & Symbols), so there the same names take the system's title style, by rule and never by hand: every word capitalised except articles, conjunctions and short prepositions inside the name ("Add to Playlist…", "Save Playlist As…", "Go to the Beginning"). The name of a film or a file is never recapitalised, anywhere. A button that starts work keeps its action as its name while busy. Units are written with the value: "250 ms", "+6 dB", "1.25×". Errors say what happened and what to do next in one sentence. No exclamation marks, no "Oops", no "successfully".
